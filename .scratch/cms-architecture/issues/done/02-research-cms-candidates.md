# Research CMS candidates and the custom-build baseline

Status: closed
Assignee: Codex
Parent: `docs/CMS Architecture Wayfinding.md`
Label: `wayfinder:research`

## Question

Which three existing CMS products pass ATF's agreed hard gates, and how do they
compare with a narrowly scoped custom CMS/ATF Org Backend option under the
agreed evidence standard and evaluation priorities?

## Comments

- 2026-08-19: Claimed by Codex for an authoritative-source comparison of three
  viable CMS products and the narrow custom CMS/ATF Org Backend baseline.
- 2026-08-19: Research artifact: [ATF CMS candidate comparison](../research/cms-candidate-comparison.md).
- 2026-08-19, resolution: Payload CMS, WordPress, and Drupal pass the recorded
  gates without depending on an unconfirmed grant or a feature-specific paid
  license. Payload is the strongest typed/headless and broader-backend fit;
  WordPress minimizes custom CMS ownership for the narrow news scope; Drupal
  supplies the broadest packaged workflow but is operationally oversized. A
  narrow custom CMS remains possible, but has the highest three-year ownership
  because ATF would own the editor, authentication and authorization, preview,
  revisions, migrations, recovery, and security maintenance. The evidence
  therefore carries Payload and WordPress forward as the serious front-runners,
  Drupal as the viable workflow-heavy reference, and custom as the explicit
  build baseline. Final article fields, editor reaction, headless preview, VPS
  fit, recovery testing, First Admin behavior, and concrete broader-backend
  demand remain decisions or validation work, not assumed facts.
