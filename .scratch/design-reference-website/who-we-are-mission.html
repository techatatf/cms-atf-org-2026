Advancing the development of science and technology in Africa since 1988.

ATF was founded at MIT in 1988 by African students and young professionals. Its form has changed every decade: a journal, a professional network, university chapters, and now the ATF AI Challenge. Its mission has stayed the same.

How we do it

By nurturing technologists, connecting them, and creating pathways to impact.

We identify and nurture technologists across every discipline and career stage. We build the community and knowledge infrastructure that connects them to each other and to the wider world. And we create the pathways that turn learning into lasting impact.

Our founding language said ATF promotes this work. We now say advancing, because ATF does more than advocate for it. We do the work.

Today we operate in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, and South Africa. We have also run university chapters in Benin.

Our Story

Four eras, one thread.

Through every era, ATF has connected African technologists, students and professionals, to knowledge, to resources, and to each other.

1988

Journal era · 1988 to early 2000s

A journal and book drives

African students and young professionals at MIT founded ATF to make the work of Africa's scientists and technologists visible. They published a journal with contributors from across Africa and the diaspora, and ran book drives that sent engineering and technology texts to university libraries across the continent. By 1992 the journal had about 23 volunteer staff, contributors in 15 countries, and more than 11,000 readers.

2000s

Consulting era · Continues today

A professional network

As the founders built careers at companies, universities, and institutions in Africa and abroad, ATF became a professional network. ATF Consulting put that network's expertise to work on projects that needed it. Consulting remains part of ATF today.

2012

University chapters era · 2012 to 2020

Back to students

ATF returned to its roots with student chapters inside universities. Chapters received funding, direction, access to experts, and exposure to emerging technologies such as nanotechnology and 3D printing. Six chapters ran at their peak: five in Ghana and one in Benin.

2023

ATF Challenge era · 2023 to today

The ATF AI Challenge

With sponsorship from OpenAI, ATF ran its first AI Challenge in 2023. The 2024 Challenge drew 450 students from 23 universities in Ghana and produced 22 MVPs. In 2025 it expanded to Nigeria, with partners such as Korle Bu Teaching Hospital and Esoko setting real problem statements. With support from Google.org, the Challenge now runs in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, and South Africa. AI is this era's field; the method — training, real problems, a community behind it — is meant to outlast it.

The journal era

11,000+

Readers of the ATF journal by 1992.

About 23 volunteers produced it, with contributors in 15 countries. The journal was ATF’s first piece of knowledge infrastructure, and it is why Knowledge is one of our three pillars today.

Where We're Going

Not an AI organisation. An organisation that connects African technologists to whatever comes next.

ATF has never been tied to one technology. We have moved from journals to book drives, to a professional network, to nanotechnology and 3D printing, and now to AI — always keeping the community that makes each step possible.

Journals→Nanotech→3D printing→AI→Next

AI is the field that matters most right now, and the ATF AI Challenge is where we are building the capacity to work at scale. But our work is to build pathways from learning to impact for African technologists, in whichever field offers the most at the time.

That is why we follow fields beyond AI that will shape Africa's next decade:

How We Work

Three pillars.

Each pillar has its own job, and they rely on each other. Programmes are stronger when a community stands behind them and when what they teach us gets published.

01

Community

Our oldest function. It covers alumni from every era, university chapters, the mentors and experts who support our programmes, and the professional network behind ATF Consulting. Programmes end, but the community carries on.
